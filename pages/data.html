Data Sharing

Towards a Shared Database

GibbonFamily is building the foundations for a community-governed, voluntary data sharing initiative to enable meta-analyses across gibbon species and populations.

Our Approach

Voluntary, equitable, and transparent

Data sharing is entirely voluntary. GibbonFamily will never pressure researchers to contribute data, and data providers retain ownership of their datasets. The goal is a collaborative resource that benefits everyone in the network — contributors and users alike.

We are currently in the design phase: consulting with network members about what kinds of data they collect, what they would be willing to share, and what collaborative questions would be most valuable to answer together.

🔒

Data ownership

Contributors retain full ownership of their data. Sharing agreements define exactly how data may be used and by whom.

✍️

Authorship credit

All data contributors to collaborative analyses will receive appropriate authorship or acknowledgement, following clear, pre-agreed guidelines.

🏛️

Community governance

The database will be governed by a member committee with transparent decision-making processes.

📋

Clear agreements

Every data sharing arrangement will be governed by a written data sharing agreement, agreed before any data transfer occurs.

Data Types

What kinds of data are we collecting?

GibbonFamily is interested in a wide range of data types. You do not need to share all of these — even one dataset can make a difference to collaborative analyses.

🔬
Behavioural data

Focal animal observations, social interaction matrices, activity budgets, agonistic and affiliative interactions, proximity data.

🧬
Cognitive task data

Results from experimental paradigms — including puzzle boxes, inhibitory control tasks, physical cognition tests — run in the wild or in captivity.

🌍
Ecological data

GPS tracking and ranging data, habitat use, population censuses, diet composition, phenology data.

🎤
Acoustic data

Song recordings, call catalogues, spectral analyses, playback experiment results.

📋
Demographic & life history data

Birth records, survivorship, reproductive histories, individual identification data (wild or captive studbook data).

Timeline

Building the database — step by step

We are taking a careful, consultative approach to database development. The current priority is building the network and understanding what researchers need.

1
Network building Current phase

Register interest, connect researchers, establish communication channels.

2
Needs assessment

Survey members on data types, sharing willingness, governance preferences, and priority questions.

3
Database design

Design database structure, data templates, and governance framework in consultation with members.

4
Pilot analyses

Run first collaborative analyses with founding members as proof-of-concept.

Interested in contributing data?

There is no commitment required at this stage. Register your interest and we'll be in touch when the database design phase begins.
