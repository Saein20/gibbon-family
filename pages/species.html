Gibbon Species

Gibbons comprise 20 recognised species across four genera. All are threatened; several are among the most endangered primates on Earth.

Introduction

The lesser apes

Gibbons (family Hylobatidae) are the only family of lesser apes — smaller-bodied than the great apes but equally fascinating. They are characterised by extraordinary brachiation, complex vocal duets, and, uniquely among apes, a largely monogamous social system.

All gibbon species are found in South and Southeast Asia, from Bangladesh and northeast India in the west to southern China in the north and Borneo and Java in the south. Their forest habitats are under severe pressure from deforestation, hunting, and illegal trade.

The four genera differ in chromosome number — a remarkable feature used in their original classification — as well as in morphology, vocalisation, and social behaviour.
